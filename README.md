# Information-Communication-and-Game-Theory-applied-to-Sales-Forecasting-Through-Statistical-Modeling


Retail demand forecasting during peak seasonal windows presents significant analytical challenges due to non-linear sales volatility, combinatorial inventory configurations, and strategic pricing competition. This paper introduces a tripartite modeling framework that merges information theory, statistical mechanics, and game theory to predict Q4 (October–December) sales trends within the retail mattress sector.

Using a synthesized 3-year longitudinal transaction dataset across multiple manufacturers and stock-keeping units (SKUs), the model evaluates:

Shannon Entropy to quantify demand predictability and time-series uncertainty distributions;

Boltzmann Entropy to model combinatorial microstate diversity and consumer choice multiplicity across modular product configurations; and

Nash Equilibrium Stability Indices to evaluate market pricing parity and competitive strategic convergence during promotional holiday windows.

Empirical results demonstrate that while overall sales volume and combinatorial configuration diversity expand dramatically during Q4, systemic market uncertainty drops as consumer purchasing and competitor discounting converge into predictable macro-states. This multidisciplinary approach provides retailers with robust quantitative triggers for automated inventory procurement, showroom allocation, and strategic pricing alignment.
